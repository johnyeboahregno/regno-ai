1.3 Advantages of Dual-Inverter Topology

Operational Benefits

  • Grid Independence: Plant runs on battery even during grid outages
  • Simultaneous Charge/Discharge: Can charge from grid while powering plant
  • Load Isolation: Plant power quality unaffected by grid disturbances
  • Maintenance Flexibility: Service one side without full shutdown

Technical Benefits

  • Optimized Sizing: Size each side for actual duty cycle
  • Reduced Stress: Inverters operate in single direction
  • Better Efficiency: Inverters optimized for specific function
  • Simpler Control: Separate charge/discharge controllers

Key Consideration

With the 2+3 split, your charging power (8 MW) is less than your discharge power (12 MW). This means full recharge takes longer than full discharge. Plan charging schedules around off-peak grid periods to optimize energy costs.

5. Power Flow Analysis

5.1 Operating Modes

Mode 1: Grid Charging Only

When: Off-peak hours, plant not operating

  • Grid → 8 MW → Battery
  • Plant disconnected or minimal load
  • Charging time: 10-11 hours
  • Use cheap overnight electricity

Mode 2: Battery Discharge Only

When: Peak hours, grid outage, or cost savings

  • Battery → 12 MW → Plant
  • Grid disconnected (optional)
  • Runtime: 7+ hours at full power
  • Complete grid independence

Mode 3: Simultaneous Operation

When: Grid available, plant operating

  • Grid → 8 MW → Battery (charging)
  • Battery → 12 MW → Plant (discharging)
  • Net battery: -4 MW (discharging)
  • Extends runtime, smooths transitions

Mode 4: Grid + Battery to Plant

When: Peak demand exceeds battery capacity

  • Grid → Direct to plant (via grid connection)
  • Battery → 12 MW → Plant (supplement)
  • Provides peak shaving capability
  • Reduces demand charges
