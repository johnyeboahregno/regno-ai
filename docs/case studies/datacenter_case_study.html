This case study examines the deployment of a utility-scale Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) at a hyperscale data center facility located at ITX Z&T Ranch in Texas, USA. The system utilizes 40 Sungrow ST2236UX battery units paired with 5 Sungrow SC4000UD-MV inverters in a specialized dual-inverter topology designed to provide uninterruptible power supply (UPS) functionality, peak shaving, and grid services revenue generation.

Critical Infrastructure Application

Data centers require 99.999% ("five nines") uptime. This BESS system provides Tier IV-equivalent backup power capability with seamless transfer, eliminating single points of failure while generating revenue through ERCOT ancillary services during normal operations.

Dual-Inverter Architecture

This installation employs a non-conventional dual-inverter topology that separates charging and discharging functions across dedicated inverter groups. This architecture is specifically designed for data center applications requiring:

  • Uninterruptible power supply (UPS) functionality
  • Seamless grid-to-battery transfer
  • Continuous charging while serving load
  • Grid services participation during normal operations

UPS Functionality - Seamless Transfer

The 3 discharging inverters operate in grid-forming mode, continuously supplying the data center load. During grid outage:

  • Transfer Time: < 10 ms (vs. 10+ ms for traditional UPS)
  • Supported Load: Full 14 MW facility or 10 MW critical-only
  • Runtime at Full Load: 89.44 MWh ÷ 14 MW = 6.4 hours
  • Runtime Critical Only: 89.44 MWh ÷ 10 MW = 8.9 hours
  • Black Start Capable: Can restart facility from complete outage

Operating Modes

Mode 1: Normal Operation (Grid-Tied)

Primary operating mode during grid availability:

  • Grid provides power to data center via bypass
  • Charging inverters maintain battery SOC
  • Discharge inverters on hot standby (synchronized)
  • Participate in ERCOT ancillary services
  • 4CP demand management active
Revenue Mode: Ancillary Services + 4CP Avoidance Grid: Active | Charging: Active | Discharge: Standby
Mode 2: Peak Shaving

Activated during high-demand periods (summer peaks):

  • Discharge inverters supplement grid power
  • Reduces facility demand below 4CP threshold
  • Captures ERCOT price spikes ($100+/MWh)
  • Maintains minimum 50% SOC for UPS reserve
  • Charging suspended until prices normalize
Peak Reduction: Up to 12 MW available 4CP Target: Reduce peaks during 15-min intervals Grid: Active | Charging: Suspended | Discharge: Active
Mode 3: UPS Mode (Island)

Activated during grid outage or power quality event:

  • Discharge inverters form microgrid (<10ms transfer)
  • Full facility load or critical-only based on SOC
  • Charging inverters disconnected from grid
  • Generator start signal issued (if available)
  • Load shedding automation if SOC critical
Transfer Time: < 10 milliseconds Full Load Runtime: 6.4 hours @ 14 MW Critical Runtime: 8.9 hours @ 10 MW Grid: Offline | Charging: Offline | Discharge: Grid-Forming
Mode 4: Arbitrage Mode

Opportunistic energy trading in ERCOT market:

  • Charge during negative/low pricing periods
